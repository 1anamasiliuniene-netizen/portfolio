Sound familiar?

Too many tools. You’re left connecting the pieces.

Bookings in a calendar. Conversations in email. Notes and exercises in separate files. You’re moving information, sending reminders and helping clients find what they need.

Each tool may work well. But adding them one by one can leave you adapting your practice to the tools — with more to untangle later.

I help you bring the whole picture into focus. Together, we explore how you want to work with clients, decide what belongs in the first version and build it with room to grow.

Imagine your working day

What could your practice become?

Your expertise is already the product. Together, we can discover what it could become digitally.

A clearer beginning
People understand your services, find useful content and see whether you’re right for them. New clients arrive with the information and preparation you need.
Less back-and-forth
Clients book or reschedule sessions, find their meeting link and make payments without a chain of emails. Helpful reminders take some of the remembering off your plate.
Support between sessions
Clients have one place for resources, exercises and action plans. They can prepare, stay in touch, see what comes next and recognise the progress they’ve made.
More room for your work
Keep everyday information together and reduce repetitive administration. Where it genuinely helps, carefully chosen automation or AI can support a task while you stay in control.

Your practice may need some of these. It may need something completely different. Discovering that is part of the work.

01ADHDbrain — a place for the whole coaching journey ↘

In practice · ADHDbrain

A coaching practice.
A place for the whole journey.

Eglė was building her ADHD coaching practice. A simple website and video calls would not support everything she wanted to do with clients, especially between sessions.

We explored how she wanted to work, what her clients needed and what technology could make possible. Then we shaped a useful first version of that larger vision.

That became ADHDbrain: a digital environment built around her coaching practice, with connected spaces for clients and their coach.

Clients know what comes next

Upcoming sessions, preparation and agreed action steps have a home. Clients can return to session recaps and reflections instead of piecing the journey together from messages.

Scheduling takes fewer messages

Booking and session information are connected to the coaching experience. Clients can find their next session and join the call from their own space.

Eglė can run the everyday work

She can prepare for sessions, manage client information and handle ordinary administration in her own workspace. The product can keep evolving as the practice develops.

What could having the right things in one place change for your practice?
